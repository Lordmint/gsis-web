Reliability curve — is the overconfidence flat or does it grow?

Predicted vs realized over the whole candidate pool, so every bucket carries maximum sample. A roughly constant negative gap means one flat correction would fix the labels. A gap that widens with confidence means the model's tail is under-dispersed — too few surprises — and the fix belongs in the variance, not the level. These need different remedies, so the shape decides what to change.

Everything above was measured on the Elo-from-strength route, because that is the only route the committed history can replay. Draws — the biggest failure mode, and why avoiding them backfires

Draws are the gate's largest failure class: 62% of every miss is a drawn game, against 38% defeats. That is more than twice the losses, and until now the harness threw the draw column away entirely — the win-pick gate only ever reads the strong side's win probability. So the harness now records the full 1X2 split on both routes and asks the only question that matters: is draw risk avoidable, or is it actually protective?

Where the bias does matter — and why it is not shipped. A drawn game is a non-loss, so draw risk is not a defect to filter out of the bet selection; it is the single largest component of what already keeps losses low. The real cost sits in market selection, not pick selection: on a level-ball Asian handicap a draw is a push, and on a ¼ line it is a half-stake refund, so the draw probability is a direct input to whether the recommended handicap is the right side. The DC route under-predicts draws by -1.6pp pooled and the Elo route by -4.6pp, so that input is biased low on both routes. Historical odds were never stored, so the money impact of correcting it cannot be replayed and measured here — the same honest limit that keeps the shrinkage prior out. Measured, documented, not wired; it is exactly the gap the prospective prediction ledger exists to close.

Intervention comparison at equal coverage — smarter, or just smaller?

Precision on its own cannot rank a selection rule: publish five picks and the average always looks better, which is exactly why a blunt global lift "proves" itself in the sweep above. Recall on its own is worthless — publish everything and you capture every win while winning nothing. So every rule below is measured twice: as published, and trimmed to the incumbent's pick count (highest calibrated probability first, the order the live card uses). At identical coverage, more wins at the same-or-better precision means the rule is genuinely smarter; fewer wins means it only threw value away.
