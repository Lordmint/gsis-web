Reliability curve — is the overconfidence flat or does it grow?

Predicted vs realized over the whole candidate pool, so every bucket carries maximum sample. A roughly constant negative gap means one flat correction would fix the labels. A gap that widens with confidence means the model's tail is under-dispersed — too few surprises — and the fix belongs in the variance, not the level. These need different remedies, so the shape decides what to change.

Draws — the biggest failure mode, and why avoiding them backfires

Draws are the gate's largest failure class: 73% of every miss is a drawn game, against 27% defeats. That is more than twice the losses, and until now the harness threw the draw column away entirely — the win-pick gate only ever reads the strong side's win probability. So the harness now records the full 1X2 split on both routes and asks the only question that matters: is draw risk avoidable, or is it actually protective?

Where the bias does matter — and why it is not shipped. A drawn game is a non-loss, so draw risk is not a defect to filter out of the bet selection; it is the single largest component of what already keeps losses low. The real cost sits in market selection, not pick selection: on a level-ball Asian handicap a draw is a push, and on a ¼ line it is a half-stake refund, so the draw probability is a direct input to whether the recommended handicap is the right side. The DC route under-predicts draws by 2.0pp pooled and the Elo route by -0.9pp, so that input is biased low on both routes. Historical odds were never stored, so the money impact of correcting it cannot be replayed and measured here — the same honest limit that keeps the shrinkage prior out. Measured, documented, not wired; it is exactly the gap the prospective prediction ledger exists to close.

Intervention comparison at equal coverage — smarter, or just smaller?

Precision on its own cannot rank a selection rule: publish five picks and the average always looks better, which is exactly why a blunt global lift "proves" itself in the sweep above. Recall on its own is worthless — publish everything and you capture every win while winning nothing. So every rule below is measured twice: as published, and trimmed to the incumbent's pick count (highest calibrated probability first, the order the live card uses). At identical coverage, more wins at the same-or-better precision means the rule is genuinely smarter; fewer wins means it only threw value away.

Reverse fixture — asymmetric head-to-head (is it real, and what is it worth?)

A "reverse fixture" is where the side we are about to call a favourite has historically failed to beat this specific opponent, even though that opponent is ranked far lower. It is the one head-to-head effect that is not already implied by team strength: read from the table below, the DC goal rates price the two groups at -1.9pp apart, while they actually realize -7.9pp apart — so the flag carries information the model does not have. Only meetings that started before each kickoff are read, so this is honest in replay and identical live.

What this harness does not measure (documented, not silent)

  • EV/odds gate not replayed here: prices are now STORED (match_odds, 2026-10-04) but ESPN only retains them for the current season (measured: 2026 90% priced, 2025 8%, 2024 0%), so a full-history EV replay is still not possible. The EV gate can only shrink the live set
  • DC route is replayed no-look-ahead from a streaming fit (same half-life/prior/caps as live, keyed by entity_id); it is not byte-identical to a rebuild of today's ratings, and it prices only fixtures where both sides were rated at kickoff
Every probability here is priced with a data-blindness tax: we have no live injury feed for soccer (−2pp on the favourite) and no prior meeting between most pairs (−1pp). These are league-wide conditions so they are stated once here rather than repeated under every match; they still reduce every published win probability.
